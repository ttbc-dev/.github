# Brand guidelines

## Character

Tokyo Tiny Builders Club makes tools for building software. Its voice is modest, specific, and plain. Serious craft and quiet playfulness belong together. Keep technical descriptions accurate; distinguish implemented behavior, experimental work, and plans. Let the artwork suggest its world without explaining the whole story in the public profile.

## World and painting

Peculiar, shaggy, unusually resourceful creatures live quietly in remote mountains. They explore and make things from branches, stones, and other materials at hand. Their tools work a little awkwardly and have an unassuming charm.

Use the expressive brushwork and atmospheric depth of traditional East Asian landscape painting: delicate broken ink contours, layered terrain, soft mist, restrained washes, and generous breathing space. Avoid polished product illustration, glossy rendering, and precise mechanical outlines.

## Color and texture

The shared landscape uses aged warm ivory paper, muted ochre, straw yellow, dusty terracotta, reddish brown, restrained olive, and warm gray. Distant mountains and mist may retain subtle blue-gray tones. Preserve visible paper fibers, faded pigment, and irregular brush marks. The approved artwork is the color reference; these words are not instructions to recolor it.

Characters retain their own palettes. In the current landscape, the creature on the left has warm ivory and reddish-brown fur; the creature on the right has silver-white and slate-blue fur. Keep that distinction rather than applying a uniform tint.

## Creatures and handmade objects

Aim for peculiar, absent-minded inhabitants with distinct silhouettes and recognizable poses. Do not deliberately exaggerate cuteness or make every character alike. Preserve their established anatomy, expressions, fur, and individual colors across appearances.

Playfulness comes from small, slightly inept constructions: a crooked three-legged stool, a branch swing, a raft, or a wooden device with an unclear purpose. Keep them incidental to the landscape. Avoid weapons, basketball imagery, and conspicuous technology symbols.

The emblem pairs a circular club composition with an ordinary, slightly uncomfortable stool. Preserve the uneven seat, crooked legs, delicate broken ink circle, aged paper, misty mountain wash, and restrained composition. The center blends into the surrounding paper rather than using a solid background field. The stool should look painted into the same world as the landscape, not like a woodworking product photograph.

Use understated, narrow, hand-painted uppercase Roman lettering: `TOKYO TINY` on the upper arc and `BUILDERS CLUB` on the lower arc. Preserve subtle irregularities in spacing, alignment, and stroke width. Avoid heavy Western serif, collegiate, heraldic, or vintage badge lettering.

## Extending the landscape

Add a new inhabitant when a new public project joins the club. Start from the current approved hero and preserve existing characters, positions, landmarks, handmade objects, and the overall composition. Make additions locally, using available space and matching the scale, brushwork, paper, and atmosphere. Keep the 3:1 composition useful at profile width. Review each extension before replacing the current hero.

Archive the prior approved image and its provenance before replacing it. Record the actual prompt, exact reference assets, available generation metadata, and the approved change. Do not label a reconstructed prompt as an original.

## Approved assets

- `assets/hero.png` is the approved organization landscape, at 1536 × 512 pixels.
- `assets/avatar.png` is the approved club emblem, at 1536 × 1536 pixels.
- `assets/manifest.json` records their checksums and encoding provenance.

Use the supplied artwork without regeneration, recoloring, retouching, stretching, or rearrangement. Proportional display scaling is allowed. Use the full square emblem for the organization avatar; avoid a tight crop that removes the outer ring. Check the stool silhouette at small sizes. The circular lettering is a detail and need not remain readable at notification size.

The supplied files were WebP-encoded despite their `.png` filenames. The repository copies use PNG encoding with identical decoded RGB pixels and the same embedded ICC profile.
